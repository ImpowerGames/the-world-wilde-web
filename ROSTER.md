# Roster — locked

**Boundary rule** (rewritten 2026-08-05, per Lovelle). An edge exists only when **both** endpoints are on this roster — that part stands. What changed is who gets to be on it: **every person the record documents as a partner, spouse or object of attraction is a node.** The old rule sent them to a person's `context_engagements` instead, and that slot turned out to be a holding pen rather than a scope lock. It hid fourteen people inside other people's files — among them a bookmaker who shared three years and three addresses with Fred Atkins, a boy bought from his father for 200 lire, and four legal wives — so a reader looking at the drawing could not see them at all. **The scope lock is now the connection, not the person**: someone joins because a documented connection to someone already here puts them in the record, which still keeps Natalie Barney's whole Paris circle and Lillie Langtry's royal one out. `context_engagements` survives for engagements that are genuinely not person-to-person — collective mentions, and pointers with nobody identifiable at the other end.

**Audited the other four connections whose reasoning contains a similar disclaimer; all four correctly keep their lines**, and the distinction is worth recording:
- `eric-ward--todd` — says *"nothing sexual is established"*, but Wilde's letter **does** assert an attachment (*"mentally spoons the boy"*). Attachment claimed, act not. That is what `second-hand` is for.
- `graham-robertson--wilde` — says *"nothing sexual is claimed"*, but the class **is** `attraction-expressed`. Consistent by definition.

### 2d. The sweep, and how it is driven (2026-08-05, revised)

**The book indexes itself, and that is the spine.** Its contents page lists two:

| | printed | repo PDF | what it gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Index of Recipients** | 1231 | **1277–1281** (5 pp.) | every letter **addressed to** each person |
| **General Index** | 1237 | **1282–1315** (34 pp.) | every **mention** — the half the old method could not see |

**39 index pages.** Reading them is the first job of the sweep, because it converts "have I found everyone?" from a regex guess into a lookup against the editors' own curated, disambiguated list. The index already proved better than our tooling three times: it separates `Georges, Monsieur` (recipient, printed 1097) from `Georges (boy)` (mentions, 1108 + 1117); it files `Harding, Amy (Miss Puss)` as a woman, independently confirming the Miss Puss finding; and it knows `G. H. Kersley`, whom a search for "George Kersley" missed entirely.

**`tools/book_index.py` is a LOCATOR, NOT A SOURCE — its folio lists are incomplete and partly wrong, by design of the source material.** The indexes are set in two columns with long entries wrapping onto indented number-only continuation lines; the OCR strips the indentation and interleaves the columns, so continuations cannot be attached to the right entry. Measured against the rendered image of printed 1232: **Clifton's 7 letters parse as 5; Douglas's 33 parse as 19, and six of those 19 actually belong to Godwin in the facing column.** Use the tool to find which index page an entry sits on, then read that page as an image and transcribe by eye. The index also carries its own errors — the `Georges (boy)` entry wrongly includes 1097 — so a correct transcription still needs checking at the cited page.

`tools/mention_index.py` (regex over the mirror) is kept as a **cross-check**, not as the driver: it catches anyone the editors' index omits, and the two disagreeing is a signal worth chasing.

**Scale:** 913 distinct PDF pages owed across 149 people, plus the 39 index pages.

### 2e. Older note on the regex index (superseded by 2d)

`tools/mention_index.py` — built for the completeness protocol. For every person on the map it lists every repo PDF page that **names** them, split into `TO` (letter headings) and `MENTION` (body, footnote, editors' note), minus pages already read. **Drive the sweep from this, not from letter headings** — reading by addressee is what lost Conder, Melvill, Pickering and a second Rothenstein letter, all on folios already read.

**Scale as of 2026-08-05: 913 distinct PDF pages owed** across 149 people.

Three matching hazards are already handled in the tool and are worth knowing about:
- **Bare given names are never swept** — `"Hank"` was matching inside **"thanks"** (348 false hits on `stafford`).
- **Descriptive nodes get their exact phrase only** — `"A 'Sea-God', Naples"` was treating **"Naples"** as a surname and returning every mention of the city.
- **Common-word surnames** (Strong, Wood, Gray, Miles, Hall, Cooper…) match on full name only.

**Every page read also settles a quote debt:** 392 of the map's 523 quotes were logged by the earlier conservative method and run ~55% short at the median. Extend them as you pass through. See `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
