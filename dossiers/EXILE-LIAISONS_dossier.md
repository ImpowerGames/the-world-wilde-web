**Sources consulted:** `letters-2000` (repo PDF, image-only scan; read as rendered page images 2026-08-03). OCR mirror used for FINDING only.

**Why this dossier exists.** The roster owner reversed the earlier call that kept this tier as a collective footnote (ROSTER.md, 2026-08-03). The stated reason: the roster already carries Charles Parker, Alfred Wood and Fred Atkins as full nodes, working-class men preserved only because a criminal court wrote them down; excluding the men preserved instead in Wilde's letters would make the chart reproduce an archival accident. Each distinct documented person in this tier therefore becomes a node. Plural or collective mentions ("the fishing population", the Napoule fisher-lads) do NOT — they stay in the collective `context_engagement` on `wilde.json`, which this agent did not edit.

**Standing caveat for every record below** (restated in each `certainty_reasoning`): the surviving record for this tier is one-sided by nature. These men left no papers; everything known of them is what Wilde wrote to his own correspondents, usually in a comic or ardent register aimed at a friend. An unrecorded outcome is therefore not evidence of no outcome, and silence about a man's own view of the encounter is silence, not consent, refusal or indifference.

## Null findings

### N1 — `grey-velvet-boy`: no node, no edge created

**Decision: null.** The passage (L3, printed 1177, verified by R11) is not Wilde reporting a person; it is Wilde reporting *being seen*. The subject of the anecdote is Aleck Ross's face and Wilde's own embarrassment, written up for Robbie Ross as comedy — "the eye he turned on me was not the sightless one. His smile was terrible."

What the record gives about the young man himself is one appositive: "a beautiful boy in grey velvet – half rough, all Hylas". No name, no age, no nationality, no occupation, no meeting, no before or after. Compare the tier's other unnamed nodes, each of which the record furnishes with something an individual can be recognised by: the Napoule fisherman has a trade, a place and a stated age, and Wilde asserts an engagement; the Sea-God has an institution and a declaration of love; the Café d'Egypte Egyptian has a workplace and a repeated encounter. The grey-velvet boy has a garment.

**The decisive point is the reporting chain.** The brief flags that the sighting is reported by Aleck Ross rather than by Wilde — that is right about the *sighting*, but understates the problem. Wilde is the writer throughout; what Aleck contributes is the witnessing, and no independent account by Aleck was consulted. So this is neither a first-person record of a connection nor genuinely third-party testimony that could be weighed as such. An `alleged` edge with a `disputed{}` block would be the honest home for a thin third-party claim — but there is no claim here to dispute: nobody, Wilde included, asserts a connection between them. Wilde asserts only that he was sitting with someone when Aleck walked past.

A node here would also breach the tier's own rule in spirit: the roster owner's test is a **distinct documented person**, and the reversal argument — that the letters preserve these men as the courts preserved Charles Parker — depends on the letters preserving *a man*. This letter preserves an outfit and a sighting. Recording him as a node would put a costume on the map next to men with names, trades and ages, and would make the chart claim a relationship where the source records an interruption.

**Where he lives instead:** the collective `context_engagement` on `wilde.json` (not this agent's file to edit), where the transcription at `EXILE-COMPANIONS_dossier.md` T3 already sits. If the owner disagrees, the class to use is `alleged` with a `disputed{}` block stating that no participant asserts a connection.
