## `bradley` + `cooper` — "Michael Field"

**Passage 1 — the editors' identifying footnote (main particulars)**

> 2. The joint pen-name of Katherine Bradley (1846-1914) and her niece Edith Cooper (1862-1913). They published many books of poems and verse plays, none of which ever reached the stage. Among them were *Callirhoë* (1884) and *The Tragic Mary* (1890) to which Wilde is here referring.

— Merlin Holland and Rupert Hart-Davis, editorial footnote to Wilde to "Michael Field", [Postmark 13 August 1890], in *The Complete Letters of Oscar Wilde* (2000), p. 449.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 471 (printed folio 449 confirmed on the page image), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Bradley's and Cooper's dates; the aunt–niece relation; "Michael Field" as their single joint authorial identity.
**Note:** the editors spell "Katherine"; modern scholarship and her own signature give "Katharine". Transcribed as printed.

**Passage 2 — Wilde writes to the two of them as one person**

> Dear Michael Field, In the case of the Independent Theatre you have to rely chiefly on actors who are out of an engagement, those who have engagements being occupied or away.

— Wilde to "Michael Field", [? September 1893], in *The Complete Letters* (2000), p. 572.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 624 (printed folio 572 confirmed on the page image — the post-plate-insert offset of +52, not the +22 that governs the 1890 letter), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Wilde addresses the pair by the single male name they wrote under — the joint identity operating socially, not only on title pages.

### `bradley--cooper` — Michael Field · **proposed `self-reported`**

**Passage 3 — the participants' own words, via their diary**

> They celebrated their poetic/sexual union in several of their poems and plays, and they even boasted of being "closer married" than the more famous Victorian couple – Elizabeth Barrett and Robert Browning – because, unlike the Brownings, they wrote under one name (*Works and Days*, 16).

— Stefano Evangelista, "Michael Field", *Y90s Biographies* (2015), para. 1.
**Provenance:** https://1890s.ca/field_bio/ · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** the class. "Works and Days, 16" is Evangelista's citation to the two women's **own joint diary**, so a participant's testimony is underneath the sentence — the README's self-reported test, which turns on whose testimony the historian's citation points to, not on whether our record happens to carry the diary sentence itself.

**Passage 4**

> Michael Field is the shared pen name of two women: Katharine Bradley (1846-1914) and Edith Cooper (1862-1913). Bradley and Cooper were aunt and niece but also life-long companions and lovers.

— Evangelista (2015), para. 1. Same provenance · verified-exact

**Passage 5 — where the first-hand source lives**

> Bradley and Cooper also left a vast joint diary, which they entitled *Works and Days*. Parts of it were published in the 1930s by their executor Thomas Sturge Moore.

— Evangelista (2015), para. 6. Same provenance · verified-exact
**Supports:** the pointer target. MS runs to c. 29 volumes (British Library) plus one at the Bodleian; Sturge Moore's 1933 selection is the printed portion. ⧖ **p. 16 of that volume is the verification target for Passage 3.**

**Recorded, not made into an edge:** Evangelista lists John Gray and Marc-André Raffalovich — both already on this roster — in connection with the two women's 1907 conversion to Catholicism, Gray becoming "one of their spiritual advisers". A real roster-internal connection, but a spiritual-direction one; this web charts romantic and sexual relationships, so no edge.

### `lee--robinson` · **proposed `second-hand`**

**Passage 6**

> It is also in 1880 that Lee first met the poet Mary Robinson (1857-1944) with whom she developed a romantic friendship that lasted until Mary married in 1887.

— Patricia Pulham, "Vernon Lee", *Y90s Biographies* (2018/2019), para. 5.
**Provenance:** https://1890s.ca/lee_bio/ · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** the relationship, its character as Pulham states it, and both endpoints. Also both women's dates.

**Passage 7 — how Lee entered the Wilde circle**

> In 1881, Lee in turn visited Mary in London. Here she met many of the Robinsons' friends and acquaintances including the Burne-Joneses, Edmund Gosse (1849-1928), the Morrises, the Rossettis, and Oscar Wilde (1854-1900).

— Pulham, para. 5. Same provenance · verified-exact
**Supports:** the introductions were Robinson's — which is the answer to "why is Mary Robinson on a Wilde roster".

**Why second-hand and not self-reported:** no letter, diary or statement by either woman about the relationship was read. Pulham's one citation to Robinson's own writing ("Robinson, 935") concerns the visitors to Casa Paget, not the relationship. Lee's letters are published (Gagel/Geoffroy) and would very likely move this record — pointer recorded, ⧖ unread.

### `anstruther-thomson--lee` · **proposed `second-hand`**

**Passage 8**

> The former was co-written with Clementina (Kit) Anstruther-Thomson (1857-1921), who replaced Mary Robinson as Lee's close companion from 1887 to 1900.

— Pulham, para. 8. Same provenance · verified-exact
**Supports:** the companionship, its span, and its position as successor to the Robinson attachment ("replaced" is Pulham's word, and the year is Robinson's marriage year).

**Passage 9**

> In the years following Anstruther-Thomson's departure from Il Palmerino, Lee became preoccupied by other concerns, but in 1924 she returned to psychological aesthetics to write a substantial introduction to Anstruther-Thomson's *Art and Man* (1924), a tribute to Kit who died in 1921.

— Pulham, para. 9. Same provenance · verified-exact
**Note on register.** Pulham is cooler here than in the Robinson paragraph — "close companion", "co-written", "departure" — and does **not** call this bond romantic in the sentences read. The JSON record therefore states the pairing and declines to characterise it. ⧖ Lee's 1924 introduction is participant testimony and is the upgrade path.

### `lee--levy` · **proposed `desire-expressed`** (direction `levy`, outcome `unreciprocated`)

**Passage 10**

> Levy's visit to Florence in 1886 was pivotal because she met Violet Paget (Vernon Lee), an important figure in the aesthetic movement of the *fin de siècle*. The love poems Levy sent to Lee (e.g., "To Vernon Lee" and "New Love, New Life") are revealing, and in letters Levy expressed her admiration. The friendship enabled Levy to become intimate with Lee's circle of friends, particularly the poet Dorothy Blomfield.

— Linda Hunt Beckman, "Amy Levy", *Shalvi/Hyman Encyclopedia of Jewish Women* (upd. 7 July 2021).
**Provenance:** https://jwa.org/encyclopedia/article/levy-amy · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** the desire and its direction. Beckman is Levy's biographer and the editor of her letters, so "love poems" is a characterisation resting on the primary correspondence.

**Passage 11 — the outcome**

> Levy's sexual orientation remains a point of contention among scholars. She was, it seems, a platonic lesbian, who never experienced a requited love attachment with any of the women to whom she was attracted. In 1886, while Levy stayed in Florence, she met and became infatuated with Vernon Lee (Violet Piaget), a novelist and essayist, to whom she dedicated one of her best sonnets.

— Andrzej Diniejko, "Amy Levy: A Tragic Late Victorian Anglo-Jewish Poet and Novelist", *The Victorian Web*.
**Provenance:** https://victorianweb.org/authors/levy/bio.html · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** `outcome: "unreciprocated"` rather than `"declined"` — an absence of reciprocation, not a documented rebuff. The scholarly caveat in the first sentence is quoted rather than elided because it is part of the record. ("Piaget" is Diniejko's slip for Paget.)

**Rejected candidate:** the full text of "To Vernon Lee" (printed on both the Victorian Web page and the Victorian Queer Archive). It is a poem, not evidence of an act, and the poem's own words would be the map asserting a reading; the scholars' characterisation carries the claim instead.

**Levy's dates — a correction to the Wilde apparatus.** Beckman heads her entry "November 10, 1861–September 10, 1889" and Diniejko independently gives 10 November 1861. Holland and Hart-Davis print "(1862–89)" at *Complete Letters* p. 326 — a year late on the birth. The two Levy specialists are followed; the editors are followed on the manner of death and the *Woman's World* bibliography, and the discrepancy is recorded in `levy.json` rather than silently dropped.

**Wilde on Levy.** Beckman quotes Wilde's tribute to Levy, which she dates to an 1890 issue of the *Woman's World*: he judged that she had "ceased to hold the orthodox doctrines of her nation, retaining, however, a strong race feeling." ⧖ **Not read at source** — this is Beckman quoting Wilde, and the tribute itself is a target for a later pass. It makes Levy the rare woman here whose tie to Wilde is mutual and in his own hand at both ends.

### `barney--vivien` · **proposed `second-hand`** · `custance--vivien` · **proposed `uncorroborated`**

Both promoted from the `context_engagement` entries already on `barney.json` and `custance.json`, now that Vivien is on the roster. Source is Sarah Parker's Y90s Custance biography, re-fetched and read in full this session (https://1890s.ca/custance_bio/).

**Passage 12** — "Custance also befriended the symbolist poet Renée Vivien (1877-1909), one of Barney's former lovers." — flat statement of the Barney–Vivien relationship, and Vivien's dates.

**Passage 13** — the Mytilene plan, sourced to Barney's *Souvenirs Indiscrets* (1960) via Parker, prefaced by Parker's warning that "Accounts of this ménage are often contradictory".

**Passage 14** — "However, Vivien's roman à clef *A Woman Appeared to Me* (1904) fictionalises Custance as the 'little English poetess Dagmar' and suggests that she and Custance enjoyed a brief love affair."

**Why `custance--vivien` is uncorroborated.** The verb is "suggests"; the evidence is one scholar's reading of one novel written by one of the two parties three years after the fact. A novel is not testimony. Nothing independent supports it, and — importantly — nothing contests it either, which the `disputed{}` block says in those words rather than inventing an opponent. Parker's peer-reviewed 2011 article files the Barney and Vivien connections together as "rumoured entanglements". What *is* documented, and is carried separately, is the sustained friendship after Custance returned to London in 1901.

! **For the roster owner:** the same Vivien material still sits as a `context_engagement` on `barney.json` and on `custance.json`. Those two entries are now duplicated by real edges and should be removed. Both files are outside this pass's ownership, so they were left untouched.

### `batten--hall` · `hall--troubridge` · both **proposed `second-hand`**

Source is the Harry Ransom Center's own finding aid to the Hall and Troubridge papers (eadid 00163), fetched and read in full 2026-08-03 — the same repository that holds the Ives diaries.

**Passage 15**

> Hall's first lover, singer and society beauty Mabel Veronica Batten (1856-1916), introduced her into a circle of artistic and intellectual women, many of them lesbians.

**Passage 16**

> Batten's cousin, the sculptor and translator Una Troubridge (1887-1963), met Hall in 1915 and the two soon became lifelong partners.

**Passage 17**

> She married naval officer Sir Ernest Troubridge (1862-1926) in 1908, soon after the death of her father. The Troubridges had one daughter, Andrea, in 1910, but were separated by 1919 because of Una's relationship with Radclyffe Hall.

**Passage 18**

> During the last nine years of her life, Hall had an affair with a Russian nurse named Evguenia Souline (1904-1958?), tolerated by Troubridge despite the unhappiness it caused her.

— all: HRC, *Radclyffe Hall and Una Vincenzo, Lady Troubridge: An Inventory of Their Papers*, "Biographical Sketch".
**Provenance:** https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=00163 · verification: verified-exact

**Why second-hand.** These are an archivist's sentences, carrying no per-claim citation (the finding aid lists ten biographies at the foot instead). They are not hedged and the repository holds the papers, so this is a statement about *who is talking*, not about doubt. ⧖ **Strong upgrade path:** Troubridge's own *The Life and Death of Radclyffe Hall* (1961), her 131 volumes of diaries in the same collection, and Glasgow's *Your John: The Love Letters of Radclyffe Hall* (1997) are all participant testimony about this pair.

**Also verified here:** Hall's self-description — "[s]elf-identifying as a sexual 'invert,' Hall adopted a masculine appearance and the name John" — used in `hall.json`.

## Part 3 — recorded nulls

**`hall--ives` — NO EDGE CREATED.** The brief for this pass stated that Ives "corresponded with" Radclyffe Hall. Nothing read this session supports that, and two checks argue against it:

1. The HRC's own finding aid to the **George Cecil Ives Papers** (eadid 00060, fetched and read in full 2026-08-03) names the Series I correspondents — Edward Carpenter, Havelock and Edith Ellis, Augustus Hare, E. B. H. Lacon, W. D. Morrison, C. M. North, Edward Westermarck, W. H. Wilkins, "and others" (with Laurence Housman in the Miscellaneous series). **Radclyffe Hall is not among them, and neither is Troubridge.**
2. The one attested Ives↔Hall link in the repo is Matt Cook, *Queer Domesticities* (2014), p. 108, already transcribed at-page in `IVES-DOMESTICITIES_dossier.md` (Passage 26): the Order "may have had some women members (Radclyffe Hall and Una, Lady Troubridge seem to have been considered)". That is a double hedge — "may have had", "seem to have been considered" — and the earlier researcher's flag on it stands: **it will not carry an edge.**

Web sources repeat that Ives's diary records a meeting with Hall whom he disliked, but no diary volume or date was reachable, so nothing was recorded. ⧖ **Open question for the HRC trip:** search the Ives diary index for Hall; the two collections sit in the same reading room.

**`levy--wilde` — NO EDGE.** Real and mutual, but professional: Wilde solicited her fiction for the *Woman's World*, published her across 1887–89, and printed a tribute after her death. Nowhere near the `desire-expressed` bar. Carried in `levy.json` prose.

**`bradley--wilde` / `cooper--wilde` / `lee--wilde` — NO EDGES.** Wilde corresponded with "Michael Field" about theatre and their verse dramas; Lee met him in 1881 and wrote about him waspishly to third parties. Literary-social, not romantic. Carried in prose.

**`batten--troubridge` — NO EDGE.** They were cousins. Kinship, recorded in both bios.
