## parker-charles ↔ wilde — Crown witness; sworn sexual testimony; conviction

**Passage 1 — first meeting and first Savoy night (examination-in-chief, second trial, day 1, 26 Apr 1895)**

> Charles Parker, examined by Mr. C. F. Gill—I am 21 years of age. I have a brother, William. I have been engaged as a valet and my brother as a groom. At the beginning of 1893 I was out of employment. I remember one day at that time being with my brother at the St. James's Restaurant, in the bar. While there Taylor came up and spoke to us. He was an entire stranger. He passed the compliments of the day, and asked us to have a drink. We got into conversation with him. He spoke about men. [p. 191/192] … And then?—Subsequently Wilde said to me. "This is the boy for me! Will you go to the Savoy Hotel with me?" I consented, and Wilde drove me in a cab to the hotel. Only he and I went, leaving my brother and Taylor behind. At the Savoy we went first to Wilde's sitting room on the second floor. More drink was offered you there?—Yes, we had liqueurs. Wilde then asked me to go into his bedroom with him.

— Hyde, *Trials* (1948), pp. 191–192.

**Method notes for the next pass.**

1. **The IIIF width cap is 1200.** `full/1400,/` returns `403 Forbidden — Requests for scales in excess of 100% are not allowed.` Use `full/1200,/`, then crop-and-upscale locally when a punctuation mark needs settling (done here for "said to me." and "boy for me!" on p. 192).
2. **In the djvu OCR stream, a folio marks the FOOT of its own page — the text that follows it belongs to the NEXT page.** Hyde's Notable British Trials setting puts the running head and the marginal witness-name at the top and the folio at the bottom, and the OCR emits them in that order. So a stream reading `… latchkey. We 201 Oscar Wilde. Alfred Wood went up to a bedroom …` means p. 201 *ends* on "We" and p. 202 *begins* on "went up". Reading a folio as the head of the following text puts every such attribution one page early. The earlier passes had this right; it is written down here so it stays right.
3. **The OCR collapses to searchable text only after normalizing.** The stream double-spaces between words and hyphenates across line breaks, so `"Charles Parker"` returns zero hits against the raw file. First strip hyphen-plus-line-break sequences (joining the split word), then collapse every run of whitespace to a single space; search against that normalized copy, not the raw stream.
