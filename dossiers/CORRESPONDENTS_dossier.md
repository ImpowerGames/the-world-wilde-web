— Holland & Hart-Davis, editorial note, Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1109 n.1 (footnote begins on 1109, concludes at the foot of 1110).

### W1 — Wilde's first surviving letter to him (printed 1109; postmark 14 December 1898)

>

>

>
> Your letter of last summer gave me great pleasure.   Pray let me know all about yourself.   Who are you?   What are you doing or going to do?   Send me your photograph.
>
> Write to me at the above address, and direct the envelope to *Sebastian Melmoth*, a fantastic name that I shall explain to you some day.   Sincerely yours

— Wilde to Louis Wilkinson, [postmark 14 December 1898], Hôtel d'Alsace [Paris]. Heading as printed: "To Louis Wilkinson / MS Hyde". Signed "OSCAR WILDE". Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1109.

# POLLITT

## wilde — reciprocal photograph correspondence, Dec 1898 – 1899 (class proposed below after all letters read)

### P1 — the Norfolk-suit photograph letter, and the Green Window (printed 1106–07; 3 December 1898)

> Dear Mr Pollitt, Thank you so much for the two photographs you have so kindly sent me.   I like the little one, in the Norfolk suit, best: it looks young and nice: the other, the one by Hollyer, looks to me like the portrait of an Oxford Dean – troubled and affected and ineffectual: not a bit like you, I am sure.   Mrs Leverson, a recognised authority for the colour of young men's hair, assured me you were quite golden, and I have always thought of you as a sort of gilt sunbeam masquerading in clothes, but, no doubt, you are Protean, and have many forms.   When you come to Paris pray be golden entirely, and leave your more serious aspect in charge of Smithers, who needs, indeed, some stern monitor for his manner of living.
>
> No: don't come to the Hôtel d'Alsace; it is a poor little Bohemian hotel, only suited for those Sybarites who are exiled from Sybaris.   It would be a poor background for you, and background is so much.   Go to some purple place, which will go with your hair: and bring a green window with you that you may look out on life in proper fashion: attitude is all.   Ever yours

— Wilde to H. C. Pollitt, 3 December 1898, Paris. Heading as printed: "To H. C. Pollitt / MS Princeton". Signed "OSCAR WILDE". Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), pp. 1106–07.
**Provenance:** repo PDF pp. 1152–53 (printed folios 1106 and 1107 confirmed), read as page images 2026-08-03; the letter runs over the page break at "young men's | hair" · verification: verified-exact (whole letter, complete)
**Supports:** (a) Pollitt sent **two** photographs, unprompted on the page — the reciprocity is Pollitt-initiated here; (b) the Norfolk-suit photograph — "it looks young and nice"; (c) the second is by **Frederick Hollyer** (editors' n. 3 at 1106: fine-art photographer, 1837–1933, who photographed Constance and Cyril Wilde); (d) **Wilde has never seen him** — he takes the colour of Pollitt's hair on Ada Leverson's authority and says "not a bit like you, I am sure"; (e) an invitation to Paris ("When you come to Paris pray be golden entirely") which Wilde immediately steers away from his own hotel.
**Editors' note 1 at printed 1107, quoted in full (one sentence):** "Pollitt's Kendal home was called The Green Window."

### T-R1 — shared queer-circle gossip as the default idiom (printed 1107–08; postmark 6 December 1898)

> My dear Reggie, Sir John arrived long after midnight on Saturday from St Malo, after a journey of about eight hours, and four *changes de voiture*.   He found his little friend Joseph with a black eye and a swollen nose, caused by intoxication and a political discussion.   Joseph also left him on Sunday morning, and did not appear till the next day, having had vine-leaves in his hair.   He is a little Dionysiac, and the conversation of Sir John, which is chiefly composed of good advice, drives him to drink. … I am glad to say Sir John is getting cured of his infatuation: and I have begged him never again to try to have a good influence: it simply drives happy bright-eyed lads to *delirium tremens*.
>
> Your little friend Alphonse was arrested last night for *chantage*.   He demanded fifteen francs, and was only given ten and a cab-fare, so on being expelled from the house he made a scene and was taken up.   There is much joy amongst his friends, as his general conduct did not meet with approval.   It is a pity he always wanted to behave badly; it gave him a demoniac pleasure.   He was quite an imp, though attractive in love-scenes.
>
> Hylton is here with his boy Herbert. …
>
> Do send me a line from time to time.   Ever yours

— Wilde to Reginald Turner, Tuesday [postmark 6 December 1898], Hôtel d'Alsace. Heading as printed: "To Reginald Turner / MS Hyde". Signed "OSCAR". Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), pp. 1107–08.

### P5 — classification note

Four letters, all Wilde's, to a man he had never seen; the whole correspondence organised around asking for, receiving and appraising photographs of him. **Direction is Wilde's** and rests on acts, not tone (P0b: he asks first, unprompted). **Outcome is unknown** and cannot be otherwise: Pollitt answered, sent at least four photographs, signed himself "Jerome", kept the letters and accepted two inscribed books — real reciprocity of a kind — but no word of his survives in this edition, and no meeting is recorded. `romantic-nonsexual` was considered and rejected: it needs a documented courtship with two participants on the page, and only one is here. The countervailing read is live and belongs to Lovelle: the README warns that Wilde's epistolary extravagance is not by itself an edge, and the ANNOTATIONS-TRIAGE pass judged Pollitt context-engagement-worthy rather than edge-worthy on exactly that ground.

**Supports:** the third photograph beat, and **the first shift in the signature — "Your friend"** where the two previous letters had "Sincerely yours".

### W6 — Ross sent to look at him, and the further-inventions footnote (printed 1127; 25 February 1899)

> If you are down at Oxford, do go to Radley and see Louis Wilkinson; he is most cultivated: knew FitzGerald: and was at the first night of *Lady Windermere's Fan*!   I suppose in a perambulator.

— Wilde to Robert Ross, Saturday [25 February 1899], Nice. Heading as printed: "To Robert Ross / MS Clark". Signed "Ever yours O. W." Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1127.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1173 (printed folio 1127 confirmed), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact

### W7 — "My dear Boy", and the FitzGerald correction (printed 1133; postmark 20 March 1899)

**Locator:** Wilde to Louis Wilkinson, [postmark 20 March 1899], Gland, Switzerland. Heading as printed: "To Louis Wilkinson / MS Wilkinson". **Opens "My dear Boy"; signed "Affectionately yours OSCAR".**
**Clinical description:** Wilde thanks him for reporting that the play arrived and amused him; describes Switzerland and the Lake of Geneva at length and disparages the Swiss; then, on being told that Wilkinson loves Shakespeare's sonnets, directs him to his own story "The Portrait of Mr W. **Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1179 (printed folio 1133 confirmed), read as page image 2026-08-03.

**Supports:** **the endpoint of the register shift — the salutation "My dear Boy", which is the salutation Wilde also used to Lord Alfred Douglas, and the signature "Affectionately yours"**; the sonnets/Mr W. ### W10 — classification note

**They never met.** That is the editors' flat statement (W0) and nothing read this session disturbs it. The correspondence is epistolary from first letter to last; the surviving record is one-sided; the boy was seventeen and Wilde forty-four. Direction is Wilde's on the strength of documented acts — three photograph requests (W1, W2, W5), the reports to Turner and Ross (W3, W4), and sending Ross to look at him (W6). Outcome is unknown and cannot be otherwise. **The strongest argument against the edge**, and it is a real one: the approach ran the other way — Wilkinson manufactured a dramatic society and at least one further false claim to get Wilde's attention (W6), and the ANNOTATIONS-TRIAGE pass judged him context-engagement-worthy rather than edge-worthy because a correspondence between people who never met fails the usual bar. `desire-expressed` edges are hidden by default on the map and are a lead pool, not the documented web; demoting this to a `wilde` context engagement is a clean and defensible alternative. Lovelle's call.

### H3 — classification note: **null**

The complete evidence is H1 and H2: Douglas's 1929 memoir calling him "a charming boy … well known to Oscar", and Wilde four years after the trials sending him a book and asking Turner "How is that dear boy? ### T-R5 — CLASSIFICATION: the null is re-affirmed

The brief offered two dispositions: `alleged` + `disputed{}` if a secondary tradition asserts romance, or re-affirmation of the null with the new material logged. **Evidence decides for the null, on one line of reasoning: `alleged` requires a `disputed{}` block naming who asserts the romance, and after this session's reads there is still no named source — primary or secondary — that asserts one; a `disputed{}` block would have to be filled with the hypothesis itself, which is exactly the laundering the README forbids.**

What the record now holds, taken together: devoted deathbed nursing in Turner's own hand (ADEY-GILBERT-TURNER dossier, T2a–T2c); presence at the death and in the funeral procession; a teasing-affectionate register sustained to the end; sustained affectionate correspondence with "Ever affectionately yours" as the standing sign-off (T-R2); shared queer-circle gossip as the default idiom, requiring no explanation between them (T-R1, T-R4); and a three-day visit to Berneval ranked below Ross's week and Fothergill's six days (T-R3). That is a portrait of the closest kind of friendship inside a queer social world — and **every erotic subject in the whole correspondence is third-party.** Nothing read across two sessions has Wilde addressing Turner as an object of his own desire.

Per the README's own warning, ardour on the page is not by itself an edge, and this material is not even ardour directed at Turner. **No `companionship` edge was created**, because this map records romantic and sexual connection and the taxonomy has no non-romantic class; inventing one would misrepresent the map's scope. The devotion is fully preserved in `turner.json`'s bio and in the two dossiers.
