## wilde — wife; courtship 1881–83, marriage 29 May 1884, two sons, separation after 1895, her death 1898

**Passage 1 — Constance's main particulars (editors' footnote at her first appearance)**

> Constance Mary Lloyd (1858–98) was the daughter of Horace Lloyd, QC (1828–74).   At this time she was staying with her maternal grandmother, Mrs Atkinson.   Constance had first met Wilde in London two years earlier …

— Holland & Hart-Davis, editorial note, *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 221 n. 1.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 243 (printed p. 221, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Born 1858, died 1898; daughter of Horace Lloyd QC; first met Wilde in London 1881.

**Passage 2 — Constance on Wilde, 7 June 1881 (her letter to Otho, quoted inside the same editorial note)**

> O. W. came yesterday at about 5.30 (by which time I was shaking with fright!) and stayed for half an hour, begged me to come and see his mother again soon, which little request I need hardly say I have kept to myself.   I can't help liking him, because when he's talking to me alone he's never a bit affected, and speaks naturally, excepting that he uses better language than most people.

— Constance Lloyd to Otho Holland Lloyd, 7 June 1881, 100 Lancaster Gate (MS Holland, M.), as printed in the editorial note, *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 221 n. 1.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 243 (printed p. 221, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Acquaintance and attraction from June 1881, in Constance's own voice.

**Passage 3 — the engagement, announced by Constance, 26 November 1883**

> My dearest Otho, Prepare yourself for an astounding piece of news!   I am engaged to Oscar Wilde and perfectly and insanely happy.   I am sure you will be glad because you like him, and I want you now to do what has hitherto been my part for you, and make it all right. …  Now that he is gone, I am so dreadfully nervous over my family; they are so cold and practical. …  I won't stand opposition, so I hope they won't try it.   Ever your loving sister
>
> CONSTANCE M. LLOYD

— Constance Lloyd to Otho Holland Lloyd, 26 November 1883, 1 Ely Place, Dublin. *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 222.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 244 (printed p. 222, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Engagement 26 November 1883, during Wilde's Dublin lecture visit — Constance's own announcement.

**Passage 4 — the wedding, 29 May 1884**

> My dear Milner, I am going to be married tomorrow – quite privately – but would be so glad to see you at the church and afterwards at 100 Lancaster Gate.   Enclosed ticket.   Yours
>
> OSCAR WILDE

— Wilde to Alfred Milner, [28 May 1884], [London]. *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 227.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 249 (printed p. 227, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact

**Passage 5 — honeymoon, Paris, June 1884 (Constance's voice)**

> My dearest Otho, I had of course forgotten your address so could not write to you until Auntie sent it to me this morning. …  The wedding seems to me to have gone off very well and Paris is charming: lovely weather till yesterday evening when a sudden storm of wind and rain broke over us in the afternoon and passed in the night. … [p. 229:] Of course I need not tell you that I am very happy, enjoying my liberty enormously. …  My dress creates a sensation in Paris. …  Ever your loving sister
>
> CONSTANCE WILDE
>
> Oscar is out, so I can give you no message from him.

— Constance Wilde to Otho Holland Lloyd, 3 June 1884, Hôtel Wagram, rue de Rivoli, Paris. *Complete Letters* (2000), pp. 227–9.
**Provenance:** repo PDF pp. 249–51 (printed pp. 227–9, folios checked), read as page images 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Honeymoon at the Hôtel Wagram, Paris, early June 1884; first letter signed "Constance Wilde". (A joint letter, "Constance and Oscar Wilde to Otho Holland Lloyd", 25 June 1884, follows at p. 229.)

**Passage 6 — Wilde's one surviving substantive letter to Constance, 16 December 1884**

> Dear and Beloved, Here am I, and you at the Antipodes.   O execrable facts, that keep our lips from kissing, though our souls are one.
>
> What can I tell you by letter?   Alas! nothing that I would tell you.   The messages of the gods to each other travel not by pen and ink and indeed your bodily presence here would not make you more real: for I feel your fingers in my hair, and your cheek brushing mine.   The air is full of the music of your [p. 242:] voice, my soul and body seem no longer mine, but mingled in some exquisite ecstasy with yours.   I feel incomplete without you.   Ever and ever yours
>
> OSCAR

— Wilde to Constance Wilde, Tuesday [Postmark 16 December 1884], The Balmoral, Edinburgh (Wilde on a lecture tour). *Complete Letters* (2000), pp. 241–2.
**Provenance:** repo PDF pp. 263–4 (printed pp. 241–2, folios checked), read as page images 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact (page-break bridged)
**Supports:** Passionate marital intimacy seven months into the marriage; the only substantive surviving letter (see next).

**Passage 7 — why only three letters survive (editors' note)**

> Except for the brief notes on pp.633 and 637, this is the only letter from Wilde to his wife which is known to have survived.   The rest were almost certainly destroyed by her family or her executors after her death.

— Holland & Hart-Davis, editorial note, *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 241 n. 6.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 263 (printed p. 241, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Only three Wilde-to-Constance letters survive (pp. 241–2, 633, 637); the rest destroyed after her death. (Matches the Index of Recipients entry "Wilde, Constance, 241, 633, 637", printed p. 1235 region, seen in the OCR mirror — mirror used for finding only.)

**Passage 8 — the two trial-period notes, February and April 1895**

> Dear Constance, I think Cyril better *not* come up.   I have so telegraphed to Mr Badley.
>
> I am coming to see you at nine o'clock.   Please be in – it is important.   Ever yours

— Wilde to Constance Wilde, [? February 1895], Hotel Avondale, Piccadilly. *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 633.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 685 (printed p. 633, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Contact during the Queensberry crisis; Cyril a boarder at Bedales (n. 3: "J. H. Badley, headmaster of Bedales School, Haywards Heath, Sussex, where Cyril was a boarder").
**Editorial note (n. 2, ≤2 sentences):** "It is impossible to date this letter exactly, but Wilde was certainly staying at the Avondale Hotel in February 1895 and may even have written this note on the fatal 28th.   It is in pencil, as is the following letter."

> Dear Constance, Allow no one to enter my bedroom or sittingroom – except servants – today.   See no one but your friends.   Ever yours
>
> OSCAR

— Wilde to Constance Wilde, [? 5 April 1895]. *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 637.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 689 (printed p. 637, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** The last surviving note to her — likely the morning the libel trial collapsed.
**Editorial note (n. 1, ≤2 sentences):** "This letter, which is in an envelope addressed to Mrs Oscar Wilde, 16 Tite Street, was clearly delivered by hand.   It cannot be dated with confidence, but the morning of the last day of the Queensberry trial, when Wilde knew he could not win, seems a likely time."

**Passage 9 — prison: her visit to break Lady Wilde's death, their last meeting**

> … as my wife has promised to settle one third on me in the case of her predeceasing me I do not wish any opposition to be made to her purchasing my life-interest.   I feel that I have brought such unhappiness on her and such ruin on my children that I have no right to go against her wishes in anything.   She was gentle and good to me here, when she came to see me.   I have full trust in her.

— Wilde to Robert Ross, 10 March 1896, [HM Prison, Reading]. *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 652.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 703 (printed p. 652, folio checked), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Wilde's deference to Constance in the settlement, and her prison visit.
**Editorial note at same page (n. 4, ≤2 sentences + quoted primary):** "Lady Wilde had died on 3 February 1896 and Constance travelled specially from Genoa to Reading to break the news to him.   Her visit was on 19 February.   It was their last meeting."   The note quotes her letter to her brother: "I went to Reading on Wednesday and saw poor O, … they say he is quite well, but he is an absolute wreck compared with what he was."

— Wilde to Carlos Blacker, Monday [Postmark 6 September 1897], Grand Hôtel de France, Rouen. *Complete Letters* (2000), p. 935.

**Passage 12 — the grave (plate caption; grave visited 1899)**

> 82. Constance Wilde's grave at Genoa.   Oscar visited it a year after her death and wrote that to see it made him 'deeply affected with a sense of the uselessness of all regrets.'   The words 'Wife of Oscar Wilde' were added only in 1963

— Holland & Hart-Davis, plate caption 82, *Complete Letters* (2000), unpaginated plate section between printed pp. 550 and 551. The headstone in the photograph reads "… CONSTANCE MARY / DAUGHTER OF HORACE LLOYD Q.C. … DIED APRIL 7TH 1898".
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 599 (unpaginated plate page), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Burial at Genoa; Wilde's 1899 grave visit; the 1963 addition of "Wife of Oscar Wilde".
