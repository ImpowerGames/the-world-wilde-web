# TURNER

## wilde — devoted inner circle; deathbed nurse (hypothesis: sexual dimension only in some secondary accounts)

### From Ross's letter to Adela Schuster, 14 December 1900 (printed pp. 1227–1228)

**Passage T1 — Turner's daily bulletins**

> I asked a Mr Turner, an old friend of mine who had been very kind to Mr Wilde since his release, to look him up every day while I was absent and send me bulletins, and Mr Turner more than fulfilled my request.

— Robert Ross to Adela Schuster, 14 December 1900. Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1227.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1272 (printed p. 1227), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Turner's devoted daily attendance on the dying Wilde, November 1900. NOTE the distancing formula "a Mr Turner" — Ross writing to Schuster introduces him as "an old friend of mine": the sentence attests care, not romance.

**Passage T2 — Turner with Wilde for the whole last week**

> It was most fortunate that Mr Turner, whom he knew very well, was with him the whole of the last week of his life.   Till Sunday 25th he was able to laugh and talk, though he got easily tired and slept a great deal and spoke constantly of coming south, so I hardly think he believed he was dying.

— Robert Ross to Adela Schuster, 14 December 1900. Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1228.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1273 (printed p. 1228), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Turner as the constant presence of Wilde's final week (November 1900). Same page also has: "Mr Turner sent me several letters to Nice reporting progress and several drives in the Bois." — and p. 1227: "Mr Turner and myself had in a brain specialist, Dr Kleiss, when meningitis declared itself."

### Turner's own voice — nursing the dying Wilde (printed pp. 1217–1219; folios verified, PDF 1262–1264, +45)

**Passage T2a — sleeping at the hotel to nurse him (end of a Turner letter to Ross, printed p. 1217)**

> I sleep at the hotel tonight in order that I may be there at 4 or 5 in the morning to try to get him to take the necessary remedies, as that is his worst moment.
>
> Did I tell you that 'Inflammation of the brain' is what he is suffering from?   The *patron* is getting frightened and anxious as to what will happen if he dies.   But he has been and is wonderfully good to him.
>
> I expect Maurice will be with me nearly all tomorrow.

— Reginald Turner to Robert Ross, [November 1900], Hôtel d'Alsace (letter end, signed "REG"). Printed in the Epilogue, Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1217.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1262 (printed p. 1217), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Turner's round-the-clock nursing; Maurice Gilbert expected at the sickroom "nearly all tomorrow" (cross-filed under GILBERT).

**Passage T2b — "it's all over with Oscar"; the painful scene (printed pp. 1217–1218)**

> My dear Robbie, I'm afraid it's all over with Oscar.   He has been delirious for two or three days and gets worse.   This morning a consultation of doctors after which I sent my wire to you. … I sat with him till 5.30 this morning.   He talks nonsense the *whole* while in English and French.   He recognises one more or less.
>
> Maurice came this morning, but does not like being in the room. … I have been watching alone while writing this, and have had a painful scene, one of many.   Oscar tried to get out of bed, and I managed to get to the bell while holding him.   He is quite gone in the mind, but recognised me again, and asked me if I could get a 'Munster to cook for him'? and added that one steamboat was very like another.

— Reginald Turner to Robert Ross, Wednesday [28 November 1900], Hôtel d'Alsace. Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), pp. 1217–18.
**Provenance:** repo PDF pp. 1262–63 (printed pp. 1217–18), read as page images 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Turner as sole night-watcher, physically restraining the delirious Wilde; devotion documented in Turner's own hand. (Editors' note 1218 n.1: the s.s. *Munster* was a Kingstown–Holyhead packet boat.)

**Passage T2c — "You dear little Jew" (printed p. 1218)**

> Just now Oscar seems a little easier.   After holding an ice bag on his head for three-quarters of an hour he said to me, 'You dear little Jew, don't you think that's enough?'   He talked to you about his play today, saying it was worth fifty centimes.

— Reginald Turner to Robert Ross, Wednesday [28 November 1900] 5.30 p.m., [Hôtel d'Alsace]. Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1218.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1263 (printed p. 1218), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Wilde's teasing-affectionate register toward Turner even in delirium; Turner's hands-on nursing (ice bag). Same letter: "I told Tucker I would be responsible for any expenses" — Turner assuming financial responsibility.

**Passage T3 — editors' plate caption (photo of Ross and Turner together)**

> Robbie Ross (left) and Reggie Turner, the two friends who were with Oscar on the day he died

— Holland & Hart-Davis, caption to plate 84, _Complete Letters_ (2000), unpaginated plate section.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 600 (unpaginated plate leaf, plates 83–86), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** editorial confirmation both Ross and Turner were present at the death (30 November 1900).

**Passage T4 — editors' plate caption (Wilde's Rome 1900 inscription to Turner)**

> Oscar on the steps of St Peter's, Rome, in 1900.   On a copy of it sent to Reggie Turner, Wilde wrote, 'A young unmitred Bishop *in partibus*'

— Holland & Hart-Davis, caption to plate 89, _Complete Letters_ (2000), unpaginated plate section.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 601 (unpaginated plate leaf, plates 87–89), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** playful-affectionate register of the late Wilde–Turner friendship (1900); no romantic claim.

# ADEY

## ross — domestic companion (hypothesis to verify)

**Passage A1 — Ross's death-account letter is addressed to Adey (printed p. 1219)**

The Epilogue's central document — Ross's full narrative of Wilde's death, laying-out and funeral, 14 December 1900 — is headed:

> Robert Ross to More Adey
> *(continued from p.1214)*

— Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1219 (heading as printed).
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1264 (printed p. 1219), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Adey as the person to whom Ross writes his most personal document, in installments (it is "continued from p.1214"). Attests closest-confidant standing in December 1900; does not by itself attest a romantic/sexual dimension.

**Passage A2 — "the blessed rosary which you gave me" (printed p. 1221)**

> Around his neck was the blessed rosary which you gave me, and on the breast a Franciscan medal given me by one of the nuns, a few flowers placed there by myself and an anonymous friend who had brought some on behalf of the children, though I do not suppose the children know that their father is dead.

— Robert Ross to More Adey, 14 December 1900. Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1221.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1266 (printed p. 1221), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Adey had given Ross a rosary, which Ross placed on Wilde's body — personal-devotional token traffic between Ross and Adey (shared Catholic piety), intimate but not romantic on its face.

**Passage A3 — Turner assumes Ross keeps Adey informed (printed p. 1219)**

> Give me any instructions you want.   I have not told More; he is moving.

— Reginald Turner to Robert Ross, [November 1900] (letter end, signed "REG"). Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 1219.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 1264 (printed p. 1219), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** within the inner circle, "More" [Adey] is Ross's own to inform — consistent with a shared household/partnership ("he is moving" — Turner tracks Adey's house-move as Ross-adjacent news). Weak inference; kept as colour, not a class-deciding quote.

**Passage A4 — editors' main-particulars footnote on Adey (printed p. 577 n.2) — ≤2-sentence editorial quote**

> William More Adey (1858–1942). … Close friend of Robert Ross, with whom he later ran the Carfax picture gallery.

— Holland & Hart-Davis, editorial note, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 577 n.2.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 629 (printed p. 577), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-elision
**Supports:** Adey born 1858, died 1942; "Close friend of Robert Ross"; Carfax gallery partnership. Elided (paraphrase for dossier): published first English translation of Ibsen's *Brand* (1891) under pseudonym William Wilson; joint editor of the *Burlington Magazine* 1911–19; for his later years the note cites Siegfried Sassoon, *Siegfried's Journey* (1945). **The footnote says "Close friend" — it does NOT assert a domestic or romantic partnership.** The telegram this note hangs on: Wilde to Adey, [23 November 1893] ("Bosie has influenza and is very pale.   The wicked Lane has been routed with slaughter.   I have begun a mystery play.").

**Passage A5 — THE COHABITATION DATUM: editors' footnote, printed p. 781 n.2 — ≤2-sentence editorial quote**

> More Adey and Robert Ross shared lodgings at 24 Hornton Street, Kensington, and Ross's mother lived nearby at 11 Upper Phillimore Gardens.

— Holland & Hart-Davis, editorial note, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 781 n.2.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 832 (printed p. 781), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Ross and Adey shared lodgings (24 Hornton Street, Kensington) — the editors' own statement, hung on Wilde's 1 April 1897 prison letter asking Ross to have the *De Profundis* typescript made "at Hornton Street or Phillimore Gardens … under your supervision". This is cohabitation evidence as of 1897; still no romantic/sexual claim in the source.

**Passage A6 — Wilde treats "you and More" as a unit (printed p. 866)**

> I wish you were not so hard to poor heretics, and would admit that even for the sheep who has no shepherd there is a Stella Maris to guide it home.   But you and More, especially More, treat me as a Dissenter.   It is very painful, and quite unjust.

— Wilde to Robert Ross, Berneval-sur-Mer, [May–June 1897 multi-day letter; the following page carries the internal date-line "Tuesday morning [1 June 1897]"]. Wilde, _Complete Letters_ (2000), p. 866.
**Provenance:** repo PDF p. 916 (printed p. 866), read as page image 2026-08-03 · verification: verified-exact
**Supports:** Wilde addresses Ross-and-Adey as a paired unit in religious teasing; editors' note 866 n.1: "Ross and Adey were both Roman Catholics." (1-sentence editorial quote).
